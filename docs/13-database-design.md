# 13 — Database Design

## SmartMine
### Digital Operations Intelligence for Small-Scale Mining

**Document:** Database Design  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Foundation Document  
**Research Context:** Zimbabwe  
**Primary Sector:** Small-Scale Mining  

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the high-level database design for SmartMine.

The database will provide the foundation for storing, connecting and retrieving operational information generated by the mining operation.

The design will support:

- Mining activities
- Material movement
- Processing
- Production
- Equipment
- Maintenance
- Fuel
- Workforce
- Inventory
- Safety
- Expenses
- Sales
- Reporting

The database should remain practical for the MVP while allowing future expansion.

---

## 2. Database Technology

SmartMine will use:

**PostgreSQL**

PostgreSQL will serve as the primary relational database.

The application backend will communicate with the database through:

**SQLAlchemy**

Database schema changes will be managed through:

**Alembic**

The architecture is:

    FASTAPI
       ↓
    SQLALCHEMY
       ↓
    POSTGRESQL

---

## 3. Database Design Principles

The SmartMine database should follow these principles:

- Data integrity
- Clear relationships
- Avoid unnecessary duplication
- Consistent naming
- Appropriate data types
- Referential integrity
- Controlled transactions
- Historical record preservation
- Secure access
- Future scalability

The database should represent the real operational relationships of a mining operation.

---

## 4. Mine and Operational Entities

Core operational configuration may include:

    Mine
      ↓
    Mining Areas
      ↓
    Materials
      ↓
    Minerals
      ↓
    Processing Workflows

A mine may have multiple operational areas.

Materials may move between different operational locations.

These entities provide the foundation for connecting operational records.

---

## 5. User and Workforce Entities

The database should support:

- Users
- Roles
- Workers
- Shifts
- Worker assignments
- Attendance

A simplified relationship is:

    User
      ↓
    Role

    Worker
      ↓
    Shift
      ↓
    Work Area
      ↓
    Activity

User permissions should be controlled separately from operational worker information.

---

## 6. Equipment Entities

Equipment records should include information such as:

- Equipment ID
- Name
- Type
- Status
- Location
- Operating hours
- Assigned operator
- Registration or asset reference where applicable

Equipment may include:

- Crushers
- Grinding mills
- Excavators
- Pumps
- Generators
- Vehicles
- Loaders

Equipment will connect to usage, downtime, maintenance and fuel records.

---

## 7. Mining and Material Entities

Mining activity should be represented through structured records.

Potential entities include:

- Extraction records
- Material movement
- Stockpiles
- Materials
- Operational locations

The relationship may be:

    Mining Area
         ↓
    Extraction
         ↓
    Material
         ↓
    Quantity
         ↓
    Material Movement
         ↓
    Destination

This allows material to be traced through relevant operational stages.

---

- Crusher
- Material
- Input quantity
- Output quantity
- Operating hours
- Downtime
- Shift
- Operator
- Fuel or energy
- Notes

Grinding records should capture:

- Grinding mill
- Material
- Quantity processed
- Operating hours
- Downtime
- Shift
- Operator
- Fuel or energy
- Notes

These entities should be connected to equipment and production where appropriate.

## 9. Processing and Production Entities

Processing may be represented through:

- Processing batches
- Processing methods
- Input material
- Output material
- Recovery measurements
- Mineral produced

Production records may include:

- Date
- Shift
- Material
- Quantity
- Unit
- Batch
- Equipment
- Operator

The relationship is:

    Material Input
         ↓
    Processing Batch
         ↓
    Processing Output
         ↓
    Production Record

The database should distinguish between operational records and specialist measurements that may require laboratory or professional verification.

- Completion status

Downtime records may include:

- Equipment
- Date
- Shift
- Start time
- End time
- Duration
- Reason
- Description
- Resolution

This creates the relationship:

    Equipment
       ↓
    Usage
       ↓
    Downtime
       ↓

       ↓

## 15. Database Development Strategy

The SmartMine database will be developed incrementally.

We will not create every possible table before development begins.

The development approach will be:

    REQUIREMENT
        ↓
    WORKFLOW
        ↓
    ENTITY
        ↓
    MODEL
        ↓
    MIGRATION
        ↓
    API
        ↓
    TEST
        ↓
    FRONTEND

The MVP database should prioritise the most important operational areas.

Initial development may focus on:

- Users
- Mine
- Mining areas
- Workers
- Shifts
- Equipment
- Extraction
- Material movement
- Production
- Crushing
- Grinding
- Processing
- Maintenance
- Fuel

Additional entities such as advanced inventory, safety, finance, sales and intelligence capabilities can be introduced progressively.

The database should evolve with the product rather than becoming an unnecessarily large system before the MVP is validated.

The guiding principle is:

> **Design the database around real operational relationships, build it incrementally, and preserve data integrity from the beginning.**

This database design will provide the foundation for:

**14 — API Design**

which will define how the frontend and other application components communicate with SmartMine's backend.
